obtain equilibrium in the base year, and parameters were estimated by regression analysis.

\subsection{Calibration and processing of data}
\subsubsection{Adjustment of demand-side data}

First, the adjustment of demand-side data was conducted. 
In the model, if a country–commodity pair has missing data for the base year 2015, it is assumed that consumption does not occur thereafter. 
Therefore, for country–commodity pairs without 2015 data in the Food Balance Sheets (FBS), the average value of subsequent years was used as the 2015 consumption level.

\begin{equation}
qdFood_{(c,cty,2015)} = \frac{\sum_{yr} qdFood^{FAO}_{(c,cty,yr)}}{N_{yr}}
\quad \text{for } qdFood_{(c,cty,2015)} = \text{NA}
\end{equation}

where \( qdFood^{FAO} \) is household consumption data from FAO,
and \( N_{yr} \) is the number of years for which data exist.

In the FBS data, household consumption includes the amount of food wasted at the household level. 
To distinguish this, waste rates by seven commodities and seven regions were obtained from FAO (2011)~\cite{faoGlobalFoodLossesAndFoodWaste2011}. 
Using these coefficients, household consumption and food waste were estimated as follows:

\begin{align}
qdWaste_{(c,cty)} &= qdFood^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} \cdot rWaste_{(c,cty)} \\
qdFood_{(c,cty)} &= qdFood^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} \cdot (1 - rWaste_{(c,cty)})
\end{align}

For model simplification, the commodities used as livestock feed, processing materials, and biofuel inputs were limited. 
Wheat, rice, maize, other cereals, roots and tubers, sugar crops, pulses, oil crops, vegetables, and oilseed cakes were used as livestock feed. 
Wheat, rice, maize, other cereals, and fruits were used as raw materials for alcoholic beverages. 
Sugar crops were used as inputs for sugars, oil crops for vegetable oils and oilseed cakes, and maize, sugar crops, and vegetable oils for biofuels. 
Feed demand, intermediate input demand, and biofuel demand for other commodities were set to zero.

\begin{align}
qdFed_{(c \notin c_{fed},cty)} &= 0 \\
qdBio_{(c \notin c_{bio},cty)} &= 0 \\
qdInt_{(c \notin c_{int},cty)} &= 0
\end{align}

Dairy quantities are expressed in raw-milk equivalent, and dairy unit values use the same quantity basis.

The FBS do not cover oilseed cakes, so their supply and utilization were taken from the FAOSTAT Supply Utilization Accounts. 
Crushing yields vegetable oil and oilseed cake jointly. 
The crushed oil crops were split between the two products in proportion to their output values at world export unit values:

\begin{equation}
intCoef_{(a,ocr,cty)} = \frac{qdInt_{(ocr,cty)} \cdot peWld_{(a)}}{\sum_{a' \in a_{crush}} \bigl(peWld_{(a')} \cdot qs_{(a',cty)}\bigr)}
\quad a \in a_{crush} = \{vol, cak\}
\end{equation}

where \( peWld \) is the export-weighted world unit value of each product.

As a result of these adjustments, the total domestic demand changed. 
If the sum of all demand components was less than the total demand from the data, 
the residual was added as “other demand”:

\begin{align}
qdOth_{(c,cty)} &= qd_{(c,cty)} -
  \nonumber\\[-0.3em]
  &\quad
  \big(qdFood_{(c,cty)} + qdFed_{(c,cty)} + qdInt_{(c,cty)} + qdSeed_{(c,cty)} +
  \nonumber\\[-0.3em]
  &\quad
  qdTour_{(c,cty)} + qdLoss_{(c,cty)} + qdWaste_{(c,cty)}\big)
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Adjustment of crop production data}
Next, the adjustment of crop production data was conducted. 
From the FBS data, the production quantities of primary crops for each country were obtained. 
These were disaggregated into production units and production technologies using the GAEZ data as follows:

\begin{equation}
qsWat_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)} =
qs^{FAO}_{(a\_cro,cty)} \cdot
\frac{qsGaez^{GAEZ}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}{\sum_{(bas,wat)} qsGaez^{GAEZ}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}
\end{equation}

where \( qs^{FAO} \) denotes production quantity data from FAO,
and \( qsGaez^{GAEZ} \) represents production data from GAEZ.

National harvested and fallow cropland totals were derived from LUH3 cropland and FAOSTAT land-use shares. Harvested land was allocated among crops using national FAOSTAT harvested areas, and then among basins and technologies using crop-specific GAEZ shares:
\begin{align}
qlCroCul_{cty}
&=\sum_{bas}qlCro^{LUH3}_{(bas,cty)}
\frac{qlCroCul^{FAO}_{cty}}{qlCroCul^{FAO}_{cty}+qlCroFal^{FAO}_{cty}},\\
qlWat_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}
&=qlCroCul_{cty}
\frac{qlHarvFao_{(a\_cro,cty)}}{\sum_{a\_cro2}qlHarvFao_{(a\_cro2,cty)}}
\nonumber\\[-0.3em]
&\quad\cdot\frac{qlGaez^{GAEZ}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}
{\sum_{bas,wat}qlGaez^{GAEZ}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}.
\end{align}
Here, \(qlHarvFao\) is FAOSTAT harvested area and \(qlGaez\) is GAEZ physical crop area. Basin harvested cropland is the sum of \(qlWat\) over crops and technologies; fallow cropland follows the national FAOSTAT fallow share.

From the MAPSPAM dataset, harvested and physical area data were obtained for each crop, 
production unit, and production technology. 
Using these data, the cropping intensity was estimated as follows:

\begin{equation}
croInt_{(bas,cty,a\_cro,wat)} =
\frac{mapspam^{Harvested}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}{mapspam^{Physical}_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}
\end{equation}

where \( mapspam^{Harvested} \) is the harvested area data from MAPSPAM, and \\
\( mapspam^{Physical} \) is the physical area data from MAPSPAM.

Crop yields were calculated using the obtained production quantities, harvested areas, 
and cropping intensities as follows:

\begin{equation}
yld_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)} =
\frac{qsWat_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)}}{qlWat_{(a\_cro,bas,cty,wat)} \cdot croInt_{(bas,cty,a\_cro,wat)}}
\end{equation}

Food-crop yields are based on FAOSTAT production and GAEZ spatial allocation. For dedicated energy crops, AIM-ALPHA instead uses the rainfed yield from H08, following the bioenergy-potential assessment of Wu et~al.~\cite{hanasakiIntegratedModelAssessment2008a,hanasakiIntegratedModelAssessment2008b,wuGlobalAdvancedBioenergy2019}.

When feedback from AIM-PLUM is enabled, the crop-yield driver is adjusted by the change in basin-average yield associated with changes in the grid-level spatial allocation of crop production~\cite{hasegawaGlobalLanduseAllocation2017}. The adjustment is one when this feedback is not used.


\subsubsection{Adjustment of livestock production data}

Next, the adjustment of livestock production data was conducted. 
From FAO data, the numbers of livestock animals and slaughtered (or milked) animals were obtained. 
Using these, the production rate was calculated. 
A minimum threshold of 0.3 was set for the production rate; if it fell below this value, 
it was assumed that a portion of livestock was not involved in production activities.

\begin{align}
rAnimalProd_{(a\_liv,cty)} &=
\frac{nAnimal^{FAO}_{(a\_liv,cty)}}{nAnimalStock^{FAO}_{(a\_liv,cty)}} \\
nAnimalRes_{(a\_liv,cty)} &=
nAnimalStock^{FAO}_{(a\_liv,cty)} -
\frac{nAnimal^{FAO}_{(a\_liv,cty)}}{0.3}
\nonumber\\[-0.3em]
&\quad
\quad (\text{if } rAnimalProd_{(a\_liv,cty)} < 0.3)
\end{align}

where \( nAnimal^{FAO} \) is the number of slaughtered (or milked) animals from FAO,
and \( nAnimalStock^{FAO} \) is the total livestock population.

When non-productive livestock exist, the corresponding unused pasture area was estimated as:

\begin{equation}
qlPasRes_{(bas,cty)} =
qlPas^{LUH3}_{(bas,cty)} \cdot
\frac{\sum_{a\_liv\_rum}nAnimalRes_{(a\_liv\_rum,cty)}}
{\sum_{a\_liv\_rum}nAnimalStock^{FAO}_{(a\_liv\_rum,cty)}}
\end{equation}

where \( qlPasRes \) denotes the unused pasture area,
and \( qlPas^{LUH3} \) is the pasture area from LUH3.

Country-level livestock production data from the FBS were disaggregated 
into production units, climate zones, and production systems 
using the dataset from Herrero et al. (2018). 
For ruminants, the share of each production unit is its share of the national pasture area. 
Climate-zone shares are obtained from Herrero et~al.~(2018), and system shares use the GDP-based regression described in \cref{sec:param}.

\begin{multline}
qsSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
= qs^{FAO}_{(a\_liv,cty)}\cdot shrBas_{(a\_liv,bas,cty)}
\\[-0.3em]
\cdot shrCz_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz)}\cdot shrSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}.
\end{multline}
\begin{multline}
qlSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
=\bigl(qlPas^{LUH3}_{(bas,cty)}-qlPasRes_{(bas,cty)}\bigr)
\\[-0.3em]
\cdot rGrassUse_{(bas,cty)}
\cdot\frac{qGrassSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}}
{\sum_{a\_liv2,cz2,sys2}qGrassSys_{(a\_liv2,bas,cty,cz2,sys2)}}.
\end{multline}
Pasture is allocated in proportion to calibrated grass demand. The grass utilization rate \(rGrassUse\) is calculated from estimated grass demand and available supply.

The grazed and ungrazed pasture areas are then computed as:

\begin{align}
qlPasGrz_{(bas,cty)} &= \sum_{(a\_liv,cz,sys)} qlSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)} \\
qlPasUng_{(bas,cty)} &= qlPas^{LUH3}_{(bas,cty)} - qlPasGrz_{(bas,cty)}
\end{align}

Next, the feed requirements and concentrate feed share were estimated as follows:

\begin{align}
feedReq_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)} &=
\frac{feedQty^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)} + pasArea^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}}
{livWt^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}} \\
shrFeedConc_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)} &=
\frac{feedQty^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}}
{feedQty^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)} + pasArea^{HERRERO}_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}}
\end{align}

where \( feedQty^{HERRERO} \) is the amount of concentrate feed from Herrero et al. (2018).

Using these values and the FBS feed demand data, concentrate feed use was adjusted with a country-level correction coefficient \(feedScale\), based on the ratio of national feed demand to estimated concentrate feed requirements. Feed commodities followed their shares in national feed use:
\begin{multline}
qFeedSys_{(a\_liv,c\_fed,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
= qsSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
\\[-0.3em]
\cdot feedReq_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
\cdot shrFeedConc_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
\\[-0.3em]
\cdot feedScale_{cty}\cdot shrFeedGrain_{(c\_fed,cty)}.
\end{multline}

Grass demand is
\begin{multline}
qGrassSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
= qsSys_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
\\[-0.3em]
\cdot feedReq_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}
\cdot\bigl(1-shrFeedConc_{(a\_liv,bas,cty,cz,sys)}\bigr).
\end{multline}
If estimated grass demand exceeded available supply, it was reduced proportionally. Feed requirements, concentrate feed shares, and grass utilization were recalculated from the adjusted quantities.


\subsubsection{Adjustment of food trade}

Next, the adjustment of food trade data was conducted. 
In order to express trade through complementary conditions, 
the net import and net export quantities were derived as follows:

\begin{align}
qm_{(c,cty)} &= qm^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} - qe^{FAO}_{(c,cty)}
\quad (\text{if } qm^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} > qe^{FAO}_{(c,cty)}) \\
qe_{(c,cty)} &= qe^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} - qm^{FAO}_{(c,cty)}
\quad (\text{if } qe^{FAO}_{(c,cty)} > qm^{FAO}_{(c,cty)})
\end{align}

where \( qm^{FAO} \) denotes the import quantity data from FAO,
and \( qe^{FAO} \) denotes the export quantity data from FAO.

In the FBS dataset, global trade is not balanced; that is, total world exports and imports do not match. The discrepancy is retained as the signed accounting residual \(qRes\), rather than interpreted as a physical transport loss.

\begin{equation}
qRes_{c} = \sum_{cty} qe_{(c,cty)} - \sum_{cty} qm_{(c,cty)}
\end{equation}



\subsubsection{Estimation of land constraints}

Next, the estimation method for land areas unsuitable for agricultural use 
due to soil and topographical constraints is described.  
From the GAEZ dataset, area data for 57 agro-ecological zone (AEZ) classes, 
classified according to climate, soil, and topographical conditions, 
were obtained.  
Among these, land classes with strong constraints were selected 
and considered unsuitable for agricultural use.  
The cropland upper bound was obtained from the remaining suitable share:

\begin{equation}
qlCroCap_{(bas,cty)} =
qlTot^{LUH3}_{(bas,cty)} \cdot
\left(1-\frac{\sum_{aezlim} qlGaez_{(aezlim,bas,cty)}}
{\sum_{aez} qlGaez_{(aez,bas,cty)}}\right)
\end{equation}

where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \( qlTot^{LUH3} \): total land area from LUH3,
\item \( qlGaez \): area data by AEZ class from GAEZ,
\item \( aez \): the set of 57 AEZ classes,
\item \( aezlim \in aez \): AEZ classes with severe land-use constraints.
\end{itemize}

In addition, protected-area information was incorporated using the World Database on Protected Areas (WDPA)~\cite{iucnUNEPWCMCWorldDatabase2025}.  
The WDPA polygons were overlaid with basin-country units and classified into primary forest, secondary forest, other natural land, pasture, and rangeland. These areas form \(qlProt_{(bas,cty,lu\_prot)}\), which is imposed as a lower bound on the corresponding land class; pasture and rangeland share the pasture constraint.

Urban land is based on the LUH3 base-year area in the default model configuration.
The base-year value is held constant in future years unless the optional SEDAC urban-projection extension is enabled.  
For this extension, the SEDAC 1-km urban fraction data~\cite{gaoGlobal1kmDownscaled2021,gaoDownscalingSSPconsistent2021} were aggregated to basin-country units and converted to annual SSP-specific series.  
When the extension is switched on, these SEDAC series are used to update urban land in scenario runs while preserving the LUH3-based base-year level.
To maintain physical consistency of the land balance, urban area was bounded by zero and by available land after subtracting water/ice.

\subsubsection{Estimation of forest carbon data}

Forest and other natural-land carbon stocks are derived from spatial outputs of AIM-PLUM and VISIT and aggregated to basin-country units~\cite{hasegawaGlobalLanduseAllocation2017,itoWaterUseEfficiencyTerrestrial2012}. Forest growth by stand age is used to calculate carbon sequestration from annual afforestation~\cite{hasegawaCarefulSelectionForest2024}.


\subsubsection{Adjustment of price data}

From the FAO dataset, producer prices, import prices, and export prices 
for each country and commodity were obtained. 
Since the price data contained many outliers and missing values, 
appropriate preprocessing was conducted.  

First, producer, import, and export prices outside 0.1--10 times the cross-country median were removed. Outlying years within each country price series were also adjusted. Processed-good producer prices were obtained from import and export unit values, weighted by traded quantities where available.

Missing producer prices were first filled using the country's relative price in the nearest observed years. Remaining gaps were filled from a quantity-weighted world price and the country's average relative price across commodities:
\begin{align}
pAvg_{c,yr}
&= \frac{\sum_{cty\in\mathcal O_{c,yr}}pp_{(c,cty,yr)}\,qs_{(c,cty,yr)}}
{\sum_{cty\in\mathcal O_{c,yr}}qs_{(c,cty,yr)}},\\
pRelAvg_{cty,yr}
&= \frac{1}{N_{cty,yr}}\sum_{c\in\mathcal O_{cty,yr}}
\frac{pp_{(c,cty,yr)}}{pAvg_{c,yr}},\\
pp_{(c,cty,yr)}&=pAvg_{c,yr}\,pRelAvg_{cty,yr}.
\end{align}
Here, \(\mathcal O\) denotes available observations and \(N\) their number. Import and export prices were filled similarly with the corresponding trade quantities as weights. World-average prices provide the final fallback; remaining producer-price gaps use the import-price level.

\bigskip

Next, production subsidies, factor employment taxes, and net subsidies on household purchases were calculated from GTAP~10. Production-side rates use output at market prices as their denominator:
\begin{align}
rPpSub_{(i,cty)}
&= \frac{OSEP^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}+\sum_{ii}ISEP^{GTAP}_{(ii,i,cty)}
+\sum_{endw}FBEP^{GTAP}_{(endw,i,cty)}}{TVOM^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}},\\
rPpTax_{(i,cty)}
&= \frac{\sum_{endw}FTRV^{GTAP}_{(endw,i,cty)}}{TVOM^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}},\\
rPcSub_{(i,cty)}
&=1-\frac{VDPA^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}+VIPA^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}}
{VDPM^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}+VIPM^{GTAP}_{(i,cty)}}.
\end{align}
Here, \(OSEP\), \(ISEP\), and \(FBEP\) are production, intermediate-input, and factor subsidies; \(FTRV\) is factor employment tax; and \(TVOM\) is output at market prices. \(VDPA\), \(VIPA\), \(VDPM\), and \(VIPM\) are domestic and imported household purchases at agent and market prices. Production costs therefore sum to \(PP(1+rPpSub-rPpTax)\), while household demand responds to \((1-rPcSub)PC\).

\textit{Indices:}  
\( i \): production sector;  
\( ii \): intermediate input sector;  
\( endw \): primary factor of production;  
\( cty \): reporting country;  
\( ccty \): partner country;  

\bigskip

The marketing margin \(mmj\) is zero in the current calibration. Trade margins \(mmm\) and \(mme\) are estimated from the CIF--FOB value gap:
\begin{align}
mmm_{(i,ccty)}
&=
\frac{
  \sum_{cty} VIWS^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
  - \sum_{cty} VXWD^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
}{
  \sum_{cty} VXWD^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
}
\\[0.5em]
mme_{(i,cty)}
&=
\frac{
  \sum_{ccty} VIWS^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
  - \sum_{ccty} VXWD^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
}{
  \sum_{ccty} VIWS^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}
}
\end{align}


where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \( VDFA^{GTAP}, VIFA^{GTAP}, EVFA^{GTAP} \): intermediate and factor input values at consumer prices,  
\item \( VIWS^{GTAP}, VXWD^{GTAP} \): import (CIF) and export (FOB) values,  
\end{itemize}

\bigskip

The import tariff (\( tm \)) and export tariff (\( te \)) were then calculated.
Each tariff rate is the corresponding value ratio minus one.

\begin{align}
&tm_{(i,ccty)} =
\frac{\sum_{cty} VIMS^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}}{\sum_{cty} VIWS^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}} - 1 \\
&te_{(i,cty)} =
\frac{\sum_{ccty} VXWD^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}}{\sum_{ccty} VXMD^{GTAP}_{(i,cty,ccty)}} - 1
\end{align}

where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \( VIMS^{GTAP} \): import value at domestic (market) prices,  
\item \( VXMD^{GTAP} \): export value at domestic (market) prices.
\end{itemize}

\bigskip

Finally, GTAP input--output data were used to separate land, feed, processing inputs, and other production costs. The cost shares use total intermediate and factor input costs as their denominator:
\begin{align}
g\_ipt_{(i,cty)}
&=\sum_{ii}\bigl(VDFA^{GTAP}_{(ii,i,cty)}+VIFA^{GTAP}_{(ii,i,cty)}\bigr)
+\sum_{endw}EVFA^{GTAP}_{(endw,i,cty)},\\
shrLandCost_{(i,cty)}
&=\frac{\sum_{ilnd}EVFA^{GTAP}_{(ilnd,i,cty)}}{g\_ipt_{(i,cty)}},\\
shrFeedCost_{(i,cty)}
&=\frac{\sum_{ifed}\bigl(VDFA^{GTAP}_{(ifed,i,cty)}+VIFA^{GTAP}_{(ifed,i,cty)}\bigr)}{g\_ipt_{(i,cty)}},\\
shrIntCost_{(i,cty)}
&=\frac{\sum_{iimd}\bigl(VDFA^{GTAP}_{(iimd,i,cty)}+VIFA^{GTAP}_{(iimd,i,cty)}\bigr)}{g\_ipt_{(i,cty)}}.
\end{align}
The sets \(ilnd\), \(ifed\), and \(iimd\) identify land/natural resources, feed, and processing inputs. Applying these shares to \(pp(1+rPpSub-rPpTax)\) gives the base-year factor costs; other production costs are the remainder.

Feed prices \(pFeed0\) equal the base-year consumer prices. The additive residual \(feedCostRes\) reconciles the cost of the physical feed inputs with the GTAP feed-cost share. Processing input prices \(pInt0\) are scaled so that the cost of the calibrated input coefficients equals the GTAP intermediate-input cost. These input prices subsequently change in proportion to \(PC/pc0\).
